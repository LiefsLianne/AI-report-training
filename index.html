Hear the band

Cuban dance music runs in cycles of eight counts. You step on 1, 2, 3 and on 5, 6, 7. Counts 4 and 8 are pauses. Every instrument repeats its own pattern across those eight counts. Switch them on one at a time until you can pick each one out, then find the 1 with your eyes closed.

Clave
  • Clave Five strokes that never change. The side with two strokes and the side with three give the music its push and pull.
  • Campana The cowbell. Its low, open tone lands on 1, 3, 5 and 7, so it is the easiest instrument to walk to.
  • Conga A dry slap on 2 and 6, then two round open tones on 4 and the “and” after it. Those two tones lead you straight into 1 and 5.
  • Bajo The bass avoids the 1. It plays just before it and lets you fill the downbeat with your step.
  • Güiro A long scrape followed by two short ones: the steady engine underneath.

Rueda de casino

Casino is also danced in a wheel: couples in a circle, with one caller shouting figures that everybody dances at the same moment, changing partners as they go. This caller picks from the figures you tick below and calls each one a few counts early, the way a real caller does. Dance guapea until you hear a call.

Figures in the wheel

Calls you will hear in a real rueda

Dame
“Give me one.” Leaders move to the next follower on their right and bring them across with a dile que no.
Dame dos
Leaders skip one follower and take the second.
Enchufla
In a wheel, an enchufla ends with the leader walking on to the next follower instead of returning to the same one.
Arriba / Abajo
In closed position the whole wheel travels: arriba with leaders walking forward, abajo with leaders walking backward.
Un fly
One clap toward the middle of the wheel, like catching a fly ball. The word comes from baseball.

Names and details differ between schools and cities. If your teacher does a figure differently, your teacher is right for that room.
